Hereditary permutations for simple types revisited: A baby case for University Algebra arXiv CS (PL, SE, DB, DC, DS)

It is a classical result that the definable isomorphisms of the simply typed lambda-calculus with function types are exactly the hereditary permutations, and that they are generated by A → B → C cong B → A → C and congruence. We give a new proof of this result that stays inside beta-normal eta-long typed terms and uses no completeness theorem: an induction that descends through both components of an isomorphism at once. We then go one level up and prove a coherence theorem: two derivations of an isomorphism denote the same isomorphism if and only if they are equal modulo an explicit list of equations (groupoid laws, functoriality, involution, naturality and the braid equation), so these equations present the groupoid of isomorphisms, a hereditary version of the Coxeter presentation of the symmetric groups.
