From elementary processes to chaos: a discrete-time two-stage contagion model and its continuous limit arXiv Analysis, PDEs & Probability (AP, PR, NA, OC)

Two-stage contagion, in which adoption requires two successive contacts with adopters, combined with population turnover produces bistability and relaxation oscillations in Katriel's differential-equation model, but no chaos. We derive from the elementary reactions of the process, without reference to the differential equations, a discrete-time model in which the population is updated in rounds of length τ, as in elections, meetings or turns of a game: each individual makes at most one transition per round, events compete as exponential risks, and adoptions take effect from the next round. The model is a Markov chain whose large-population limit is a map Fτ; Katriel's equations are recovered as τ→0.

Zirah: A Post-Quantum True Random Number Generator Hardware Signer With an Avalanche Noise arXiv Systems & Architecture (cs.OS, cs.AR, cs.NI, cs.PF)

Zirah is a hardware signer built around an STM32F407 microcontroller, and its purpose is to find out, on inexpensive hardware, where a post-quantum signing key is exposed and what actually protects it. The device draws every random bit from a reverse-biased 2N3904 junction in avalanche breakdown, amplified by an LM358 and screened by SP 800-90B health tests, computes ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204, hedged) on the STM32 with two-share arithmetic masking of the secret-key multiplications, and adds ECDSA P-256 from an ATECC608 secure element over the same digest, once a person has matched an eight-digit digest prefix on its OLED with the PC and pressed a button. A desktop application and a command-line tool drive the board and verify its output, and because only a 32-byte manifest digest reaches the device, it signs files of any type and size, returning signed copies for PDF, Office Open XML, ZIP, PNG and JPEG files and detached records for every other format.
