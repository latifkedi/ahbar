We prove a quantum analogue of the classical moment matching method, a popular framework used to prove lower bounds in property testing. For two random variables X0 and X1 whose moments match up to the first r-1 moments, the classical moment matching method says that no classical algorithm can reliably distinguish between input instances of length N constructed from X0 and X1, respectively, unless it performs at least Ω(N1-1/r) queries. While this method has been widely explored since its inception in the seminal work of Raskhodnikova, Ron, Shpilka, and Smith (STOC 2007) to prove lower bounds for symmetric properties, it has remained an open question whether a quantum analogue of it could be proved.
