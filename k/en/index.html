Dimensional Crossover and Correlated Spin Dynamics in the Chain-Like van der Waals Antiferromagnet AgCrP2S6 arXiv Quantum & Condensed Matter (quant-ph, cond-mat)

We report a comprehensive investigation of the magnetic properties and spin dynamics of the chain-like van der Waals antiferromagnet AgCrP2S6 using magnetization, magnetic susceptibility, and 31P nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR). The magnetic susceptibility exhibits a broad maximum near 250 K, characteristic of strong short-range antiferromagnetic correlations in a low-dimensional system. A classical Fisher-chain analysis gives an effective intrachain exchange scale J/kB≈ 140 K, while a quantum S=3/2 nearest-neighbor-chain calculation yields J/kB≈ 114 K.

Quantum-Assisted Counterfactual Regret Minimization for Bounded-Payoff Imperfect-Information Games arXiv Quantum & Condensed Matter (quant-ph, cond-mat)

Imperfect-information games with a graded-reward structure present significant computational challenges for equilibrium computation, since the payoff expectations must be estimated across exponentially large strategy spaces. Counterfactual Regret Minimization over the years has proven effective for approximating Nash equilibria, but Monte Carlo variants create a large overhead of sampling costs when the counterfactual values are estimated from sampled game-tree trajectories. We propose a novel Quantum-Assisted Counterfactual Regret Minimization, a framework that uses Quantum Amplitude Estimation to reduce the computational complexity of the regret updates in bounded-payoff imperfect-information games, while preserving the convergence of its classical counterpart.

Sable: A Small-Field Sumcheck Argument for Bit-Exact Inference of Large Language Models arXiv Cryptography & Security (cs.CR)

We present Sable, an argument system and single-GPU prover for the statement that a committed, integer-quantized transformer produced a given token sequence from a given prompt, bit for bit. Sable fixes an integer function F_O with no floating-point step, in which every rounding, normalization, softmax and sampling decision is determined by its inputs, and proves it with a constraint system whose relations provably never wrap modulo the 31-bit KoalaBear prime. The proof batches zerochecks and LogUp-GKR lookups into one sumcheck per domain, and commits to every witness tensor and weight in one multilinear commitment built from Reed-Solomon codes, BLAKE3 Merkle trees and a mixed-length BaseFold opening.
