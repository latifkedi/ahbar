BridgeWAM: Bridging Video and Action Experts via Latent Bridge Queries

World Action Models (WAMs) commonly couple video and action experts through dense, layer-wise Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) attention, exposing action prediction to irrelevant visual features while enforcing matched expert depths. This coupling hinders selective visual-feature use and introduces redundant computation. We introduce Bridge-of-Experts (BoE), instantiated as BridgeWAM, which aggregates visual, language, and proprioceptive priors through 32 Latent Bridge Queries (LBQs) into a shared, limited latent space.

RAP: A Dataset for Paper-to-Poster Generation via Declarative Markup from Human-Crafted Exemplars

Making a conference poster is visual coding in miniature: an entire paper must be compressed onto a single page, which exercises summarization, visual design, and code implementation at once, using nothing beyond the paper itself. Yet benchmarks for this task in the context of visual coding remain scarce: existing resources are small and annotated only with layout boxes, so evaluation must fall back on VLM-as-judge or human checklists rather than objective render-and-compare, and the generation methods they support design layouts from scratch, leaving human-crafted posters unusable as either a retrievable design library or as verified supervision. We introduce RAP (Reconstruct A Poster), a dataset and benchmark for paper-to-poster generation as a visual coding task.

Spectral networks, finite webs, and the $T_3$ cubic differential

We describe spectral networks for cubic differentials on $\mathbf{P}^1$ with exactly three poles all of order two, following observations of Hollands-Neitzke. To do so, we give a precise definition and establish some basic properties of a class of finite webs (corresponding to BPS states in physics) in the cubic differential setting, including their non-vanishing central charge. Turning to the example, we prove that at rational angles the network is totally degenerate; the number of walls and Stokes regions is explicitly computed and depends on an arithmetic criterion.
