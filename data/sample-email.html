Boundary-Sensitive Quantum Order on Non-Amenable Lattices · arXiv Quantum & Condensed Matter (quant-ph, cond-mat)

We show that quantum systems on non-amenable lattices, whose boundaries constitute an extensive fraction of the system, host novel forms of bulk ordering with no Euclidean counterpart. Combining boundary-retaining iDMRG with an exact symmetry-fixing duality, we investigate p<5 state clock models on the coordination-3 infinite Cayley tree. We find that between the ordered and disordered phases lies an emergent intermediate phase with bulk order induced by an arbitrarily weak boundary field rather than by conventional spontaneous symmetry-breaking.

Proximity Transient Absorption in van der Waals Heterostructures · arXiv Quantum & Condensed Matter (quant-ph, cond-mat)

Transient absorption spectroscopy is a widely used technique for investigating ultrafast electronic dynamics, where transient optical signals are generally interpreted as arising from excitations within the material hosting the probed optical transition. A distinct form of transient absorption spectroscopy, termed proximity transient absorption (PTA), is demonstrated, in which the probed optical transition and the monitored photoexcited carriers reside in different materials. Using graphene/hBN/WS_2 heterostructures, photoexcited carriers in graphene induce a strong transient optical response at the excitonic resonance of a WS_2 monolayer through Coulomb screening, even when the two layers remain electronically isolated by an atomically thin hBN spacer.

The Net Present Value of the California Billionaire Tax Act -- by Benjamin Jaros, Joshua Rauh · NBER Working Papers (National Bureau of Economic Research)

This paper examines the revenue and migration impact of the 2026 California Billionaire Tax Act, a one-time 5 percent tax on the worldwide net worth of individuals exceeding $1 billion. Using data from the Forbes Billionaire List, news reports detailing the residential real estate holdings of California billionaires, and public announcements of billionaire departures from the state, we estimate the behavioral response and associated revenue loss to this new tax using several different methodologies. Even before accounting for reductions in other tax bases such as the income tax, we estimate that the Act will collect approximately $30 billion (range $28 to 36 billion), less than a third of the $100 billion projected by Galle et al.
